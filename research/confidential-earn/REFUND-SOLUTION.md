# Residual-balance investigation — no accepted solution yet

**Status (29 September 2026):** The acceptance criterion is **strictly less than 0.5 USDC** remaining after settlement; **less than 0.1 USDC** is preferred. No approach in this document has demonstrated all of the fee, residual, and privacy requirements. [HANDOVER.md](HANDOVER.md) records the accepted boundary. The sections below preserve research and rejected candidates, not execution instructions.

**Later tests and implemented guard:** [RESIDUAL-VERIFICATION.md](RESIDUAL-VERIFICATION.md) supersedes the implementation-status statements below. A conservative `starting balance − deposit < 0.5 USDC` guard now blocks signing/submission; the low-gas fork cases establish a conditional residual bound, not live relay admission. The historical-price cases still fail. No second-deposit cleanup was authorized or sent.

## Rejected: another Circle-paid deposit

The earlier recommendation to make a second deposit was rejected: it incurs another gas charge, may not fit the wallet's available maximum hold, and still returns a variable refund after execution. Do not repeat deposits as a cleanup strategy. The following local projection is historical evidence only; it is not an accepted plan or a tested mainnet relay configuration.

The stale recommendation survived because this investigation mixed proposals with results: its opening promoted a conditional local projection to a usable path, while later caveats and the handover recorded the unresolved conditions and rejection. The handover was updated without removing the contradictory opening. The prior task transcript confirms the sequence: on 29 September at 01:06 UTC another Circle deposit was recommended, at 01:09 UTC the user rejected it, and the 01:11 UTC handover recorded the rejection without updating this note. This is documentation drift after explicit rejection, not a change in the user's requirements. The technical gap was the same: a local EntryPoint success and an estimated refund were treated as progress toward acceptance without proving relay admission and the final residual together.

The previous short-lived-permit proposal was also incompatible with this Circle implementation: `validatePaymasterUserOp` passes `type(uint256).max` as the permit deadline. The runner mirrors that value. A caller cannot insert a shorter deadline into the existing paymaster payload.

For the post-deposit wallet-2 state, a **local fork** with a 400,000 call gas limit, 165,395 verification gas, 55,679 pre-verification gas, 120,000 Circle verification gas, and 35,000 post-operation gas succeeded. At the read-only 0.630915148-gwei quote, it projected a **1.376447 USDC hold**, **0.355315 USDC further deposit** from the 1.731762-USDC balance, **1.032623 USDC charge**, and **0.343824 USDC final refund**. The more conservative 410,000/125,000 call/Circle-verification limits projected a 0.361219-USDC refund. These limits have not yet been accepted by Candide on mainnet and a later inclusion price can change the result. The runner currently does not implement the fail-closed expiry and bound checks, so it must not present this projection as a live guarantee.

Under 0.1 USDC is not supported by these Circle gas-limit profiles at that quote. With Circle, the refund is the gap between the maximum USDC hold and actual gas charge, and the refund arrives after the vault call. Do not promise an absolute <=0.5 USDC balance from a single Circle operation unless that post-operation behavior changes.

## Sponsored 7702 route and its unresolved fee collection

Use an Ethereum ERC-4337 **verifying paymaster** to sponsor the existing Simple7702 wallet's vault deposit. Pimlico's documented `pm_sponsorUserOperation` signs a sponsored operation and debits the application's off-chain Pimlico balance; it supports EntryPoint v0.8. The destination wallet needs no ETH and Circle never takes a USDC hold. The wallet signs a batch of the Morpho SDK's approval and deposit calls. Its `executeBatch` reverts the whole batch if either call fails, so the wallet receives its own vault shares or neither call takes effect. The sponsor still pays gas for a failed on-chain operation.

For each wallet, read its confirmed Ethereum USDC balance `B`, choose residual `R = 0.05 USDC`, and build the SDK deposit for `D = B − R`. For the currently observed wallet-2 balance of **1.731762 USDC**, that would mean **1.681762 USDC deposited** and **0.050000 USDC remaining** on successful execution. This is arithmetic, not a tested sponsored transaction or a live sponsorship quote. The deposited amount is signed, so a concurrent inbound USDC transfer could increase the final residual; check the balance and nonce immediately before sponsorship/submission and reconcile after inclusion. A strict on-chain cap even against unsolicited incoming funds would need a balance-aware contract or wallet implementation.

The user must still pay the sponsorship cost. Collecting a fee at the Monad source or before confidential routing is **outside the accepted flow**. A USDC transfer from both destinations to one app fee address would publicly link them. Pimlico's verifying paymaster debits the application's off-chain balance, so this route has no demonstrated way to recover the fee from these destination wallets without introducing a link. Treat it as an incomplete candidate, not the recommended implementation.

Do **not** batch a USDC fee transfer from both destination wallets to the same app address. That public recipient would link them. Pimlico sees the sponsored wallets off-chain, so this route protects against an on-chain link between destinations, not against Pimlico or the app correlating their requests. Inspect actual v0.8 paymaster data and a sponsored receipt for any app-specific on-chain marker before claiming the two deposits are unlinkable.

The arithmetic gives a practical **<0.1 USDC** residual, but the fee and privacy requirements remain unresolved. No sponsored UserOperation was sent in this investigation.

Pimlico's ERC-20 mode does not solve this by setting a `constantFee`: its contract adds that fee **to the actual gas charge**, and `postOp` transfers the variable total. It can optionally redirect unused prefund to a `recipient`, but that moves USDC rather than depositing vault shares. See [Pimlico's v0.8 paymaster](https://github.com/pimlicolabs/singleton-paymaster/blob/master/src/SingletonPaymasterV8.sol) and [the inherited postOp calculation](https://github.com/pimlicolabs/singleton-paymaster/blob/master/src/SingletonPaymasterV7.sol).

Sources: [Pimlico verifying paymaster API](https://docs.pimlico.io/references/paymaster/verifying-paymaster/endpoints), [Simple7702Account](https://github.com/eth-infinitism/account-abstraction/blob/develop/contracts/accounts/Simple7702Account.sol), [atomic executeBatch](https://github.com/eth-infinitism/account-abstraction/blob/develop/contracts/core/BaseAccount.sol), [Morpho SDK flow](https://docs.morpho.org/developers/earn/tutorials/assets-flow/).

## Rejected for these two wallets: a new atomic relayed deposit helper

An atomic, gas-paid-by-relayer deposit can make the final Ethereum vault step deterministic. The destination wallet signs an exact USDC permit and a separate deposit intent. A shared, non-upgradeable helper pulls the authorized USDC, pays the relayer a fixed quoted USDC fee, and deposits the rest into the Pendle Ecosystem USDC Morpho vault **on behalf of the same destination wallet**. The relayer pays Ethereum gas; the destination wallet needs no ETH. The helper reverts unless the wallet receives at least its signed minimum shares and retains at most its signed USDC residual, normally 0.05 USDC. Each destination wallet uses an independent intent and receives its own shares; there is no transfer between destination wallets.

This is an undeployed research design. A helper used only by these two wallets introduces a public common marker and fails the accepted privacy requirement. The design below is retained for context, not as an implementation recommendation.

## Why the present Circle operation cannot enforce a hard dust ceiling

Circle pulls the **maximum** USDC gas cost before the wallet's deposit call and returns unused USDC in `postOp`, after that deposit call has finished. The same call cannot invest the refund. A smaller maximum reduces the expected refund but can make the UserOperation fail; the effective fee and vault state can change before inclusion.

A local Anvil fork of the pre-deposit state at Ethereum block 26,079,532 succeeded with signed limits of 400,000 wallet call, 165,395 verification, 55,679 pre-verification, 120,000 Circle validation, and 35,000 post-operation gas. At a matched 1 gwei maximum and effective fee, the projected refund was **0.498964 USDC**. The call and Circle validation margins over the observed usage were only about 21,000 and 12,000 gas. Raising those margins to 410,000 and 125,000 gas made the projected refund **0.539312 USDC** at that price.

A second local fork of wallet 2's *post-deposit* state succeeded with all four tested profiles. At its read-only market quote of **0.630915148 gwei** and the then-current Circle token price, the 410,000 / 165,395 / 55,679 / 125,000 / 35,000 profile projected a **0.361219 USDC** refund and a **1.401903 USDC** maximum hold. This is a useful conditional option for a cheap-gas test, not a ceiling: a lower inclusion-block base fee or a cheaper execution path raises the refund. Candide has accepted the 165,395 verification limit, but it has **not** accepted the reduced 410,000 wallet and 125,000 paymaster limits on mainnet. No mainnet operation was sent during this investigation.

For Circle-only use, a safe runner must simulate the exact wallet state, keep margins on each call, calculate the refund at the current Circle price and a range of plausible inclusion prices, and **stop before signing** if the upper estimate exceeds a conservative threshold such as 0.35 USDC. Even then, this is a best-effort policy, not a hard 0.5 USDC guarantee. A later cleanup transaction would need its own gas source.

## Atomic relayed deposit

The wallet signs an EIP-712 intent binding: Ethereum chain ID and helper address, owner, the fixed Ethereum USDC and Morpho vault addresses, deposit amount, exact relayer fee, minimum shares, maximum residual USDC, helper nonce, chosen relayer, and a short deadline. It also signs an exact-value [USDC EIP-2612 permit](https://github.com/circlefin/stablecoin-evm/blob/master/contracts/v2/FiatTokenV2.sol) for the helper to pull `deposit amount + fee`. The relayer receives the signatures and submits one transaction using its own ETH.

The helper checks both signatures, nonce, deadline, relayer, and fixed token/vault. It calls `permit`, pulls exactly the signed USDC amount, approves the vault for exactly the deposit amount, and calls [`deposit(assets, onBehalf)`](https://docs.morpho.org/developers/contracts/morpho-vaults-v2/) with `onBehalf = owner`. It checks the returned shares against the signed minimum, pays the fixed fee in USDC to the relayer, and checks the owner's remaining USDC against the signed maximum. Any failed check reverts the entire transaction, including the permit and token movements. The vault's `deposit` is callable by anyone and mints shares to `onBehalf`, so the relayer never owns the user's shares. Use exact approvals and a reentrancy guard, and do not expose an arbitrary call target or fee recipient. The helper's signature nonce prevents replay; the signed deadline limits stale fee and share-price quotes.

The signed residual is deterministic on success: `starting USDC − deposited USDC − relayer fee`. A fresh balance read selects deposit amount so this equals 0.05 USDC; the onchain residual check makes a changed balance fail closed. The relayer must quote a fixed USDC fee that covers its expected Ethereum gas plus its risk margin. The user sees that fee before signing and never has to pre-fund a gas *maximum* that later returns to the wallet. The relayer bears any difference between its quoted fee and actual ETH gas cost. The application can obtain competing relayer quotes; each wallet can choose a different relayer.

This direct-vault path needs an explicit signed `minShares` check to preserve Morpho's share-price protection. The present [Morpho SDK flow](https://docs.morpho.org/developers/earn/tutorials/assets-flow/) uses VaultBundlesV1, which mints to its transaction sender, so merely submitting its transaction from a relayer would mint shares to the relayer. The helper must call the vault directly and enforce the signed share bound itself.

I checked existing contracts before proposing a new helper. Morpho says [VaultBundlesV1 operates on `msg.sender`](https://docs.morpho.org/developers/contracts/bundles/) and [Bundler3 is deprecated](https://docs.morpho.org/developers/contracts/bundles/); its adapter's transfer-from path also uses the bundle initiator rather than an independently signed USDC owner. A generic multicall plus USDC authorization would not bind the subsequent deposit and fee calls, allowing an authorization to be used to divert the funds. [Safe's EIP-7702 variants](https://docs.safe.global/advanced/eip-7702/7702-safe) are described by Safe as experimental and unaudited, so redelegating an already funded wallet to them is not a safe shortcut.

## Privacy and rollout gates

Each wallet sends only its own signed intent to a relayer and receives only its own vault shares. No wallet-to-wallet transfer or common source-wallet transfer is added. A relayer can see the wallet whose intent it receives; privacy against that relayer requires separate delivery or a larger permissionless relayer set. A newly deployed helper used by only these two wallets would itself be an obvious public common touchpoint. This design is appropriate only if the helper serves a broad, unrelated user set, or if the project's anonymity model accepts that link. Otherwise a widely used sponsorship path without an app-specific onchain marker is needed. Do not deploy this helper for just the two experiment wallets and claim their deposits are unlinkable.

Before mainnet use, test the helper on a fork with the existing wallet state and a zero-ETH destination; test expired/replayed intents, modified fees or recipients, share-price movement, balance changes, USDC blacklist/pause behavior, vault reverts, reentrancy, and relayer nonpayment. Obtain an independent contract security review. Then run a small, single-wallet mainnet deposit and reconcile the wallet's USDC, minted shares, fee transfer, and relayer ETH expense. Only after that should the confidential routing experiment switch its final deposit step.
