## What we learned about the calculation

Circle does not accept a caller-chosen final gas payment. The USDC permit is an allowance. During validation it computes and pulls the **maximum** token cost from all five signed UserOperation gas limits, `maxFeePerGas`, its live token price, spread, and additional charge. During `postOp`, after the wallet's vault call, it computes the **actual** charge and refunds the difference. Thus the refund cannot be included in the same vault deposit. Setting the permit to estimated actual gas plus 0.1 without reducing the signed maximum makes validation fail.

For the first deposit, five limits totaled 1,285,971 gas at a 1.292466979-gwei maximum fee. At Circle's event price, this exactly reconstructs the **4.527847 USDC hold**. The inclusion effective fee was 1.118379612 gwei; about 660,828 gas before `postOp` reconstructs the **2.063812 USDC charge**, hence **2.464035 USDC refunded**. The EntryPoint's total receipt gas cannot be inserted directly into Circle's formula because it includes later `postOp` work. The verified `circleFinalCharge` functions in both experiments now model this distinction.

For the second successful deposit, signed limits totaled **1,313,760 gas**, versus about **638,383 gas billable before `postOp`**. The public trace shows 379,164 gas in wallet execution, 108,438 in Circle validation, 4,344 in account validation, and 10,931 in `postOp`. Using even those observed inner-call numbers as *unsafe zero-margin* replacement limits plus Candide's accepted verification floor still totals at least 743,676 signed gas. At the same fee and charge, reaching a 0.1-USDC refund would have required around 677,000 signed gas or less; tightening the call limit also removes an unused-gas penalty, lowering actual charge and making the target still harder. Copying a historical gas limit into the changed wallet state is unsafe.

The runner now calculates the exact next-block EIP-1559 base fee from the latest block and adds a recent priority-fee estimate. It fails if the reference block changes before signing, checks Pimlico's relay fee floor, and supports same-nonce repricing with the bundler's 10% replacement rule. This solves stale/fixed bidding but **does not** make the maximum fee equal the effective fee if inclusion is delayed or the relay imposes a higher floor. It also does not reduce oversized gas limits. An earlier snapshot showed a fee-price gap alone projecting about 0.55 USDC refund even with perfectly matched gas limits.

## Local tests and rejected ideas

The gas lab used signed operations only on a local Anvil fork. One earlier delegated-wallet snapshot failed at 370,000 wallet-call gas, 35,000 account-verification gas, or 118,000 Circle-validation gas; a tighter successful profile still had little safety margin. A later fork of the pre-deposit state at block 26,079,532 succeeded at 400,000 / 165,395 / 55,679 / 120,000 / 35,000 (call / account / pre / Circle validation / post). At a matched 1-gwei fee it projected **0.498964 USDC refund**; 410,000 call and 125,000 Circle validation projected **0.539312**. A post-deposit-state fork at a read-only 0.630915148-gwei quote projected **0.343824–0.361219 USDC refund** for those profiles. These are conditional simulations; Candide has **not** accepted the reduced call and Circle-validation limits on mainnet, and the current runner does not apply them. A local fork success is not a guarantee of the final refund.

Suggestions made after those tests were not solutions to the accepted requirement:

- A **second Circle-paid vault deposit** from wallet 2 would incur another real Ethereum gas charge, may have too little balance for its maximum hold, and still produces an uncertain `postOp` refund. The low-gas fork projection is not a guaranteed <=0.5 result. This suggestion was rejected and should not be treated as the plan.
- A **Pimlico verifying-paymaster-sponsored operation** could let the wallet deposit nearly all its USDC and leave a chosen residual, but it debits the application's off-chain Pimlico balance. Fee collection was unresolved. Charging at the Monad source was explicitly rejected; a common app fee address paid by both Ethereum wallets creates a visible link. No sponsored mainnet operation was sent. Pimlico's ERC-20 paymaster `constantFee` is added **on top of** variable actual gas, rather than replacing it with a flat total.
- A **new relayer/deposit helper** could take a fixed fee and deposit into Morpho `onBehalf` of the wallet, but a helper used by just these two wallets would itself link them publicly. It also needs contract implementation, audit, and separate share-price protection. It is a research design, not a deployed or acceptable solution for this experiment.
- Merely sending USDC to the vault contract does not mint shares. Morpho's current SDK bundler mints to its transaction sender, so relaying that SDK transaction directly from another address would give the relayer the shares.
